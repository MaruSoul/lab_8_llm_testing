The oldest surviving photograph taken with a camera was made by Joseph Nicephore Niepce around 1826 or 1827, and it
needed an exposure of many hours. In 1839 Louis Daguerre presented the daguerreotype, the first practical photographic
process available to the public. In 1888 the Kodak company released a simple box camera sold with the slogan
"You press the button, we do the rest", which made photography popular with amateurs. The first prototype of a digital
camera was built in 1975 by Steven Sasson, an engineer at Kodak. It recorded black-and-white images of 0.01 megapixels.
